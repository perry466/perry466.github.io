I read a book at twenty-two that I thought was about ambition. I read it again last month, at thirty-two, and it turned out to be about the cost of ambition. Same words.

## The book did not change

Nothing was rewritten. No new edition, no added preface. The difference is entirely in what I brought to it: ten years of watching people — including myself — get some of the things they wanted.

That is the awkward truth about re-reading. You cannot plan the second read. You can only wait until you have enough life for the book to mean something else.

## What the second read cost

Two months, for a book I finished in a week the first time.

- I stopped at paragraphs that had once seemed like filler.
- I noticed the minor characters, who exist mostly to be ignored.
- I found the author's judgment in the sentence rhythm, not in the plot.

> A book read slowly is not a better book. It is a different book, and you are a different reader.

## The practical part

Two habits made the second read possible. Neither is a system.

1. Underline almost nothing. If everything is marked, nothing is. I marked eleven passages in two months.
2. Write one line per sitting, not per chapter. I finished with twenty-three lines, and about six of them are worth keeping.

## What I would tell someone at twenty-two

That the book is not about what it seems to be about. That this is not a criticism. That the same is true of most things you will read, and of a few people you will meet.
