Every book I finish gets one line: date, title, and a single sentence about what stayed. Nothing else.

## The rule that made it work

One line. Not a summary, not a rating, not a list of quotes. A summary is a task; a line is a note to myself.

Because the bar is low, it actually gets done. Because the bar is low, the line has to be honest — there is no room for sounding smart.

## What the log is good for

- It remembers books I would otherwise have forgotten I read.
- Reading the log in order shows which ideas kept coming back over a year.
- It kills the illusion of reading a lot: twenty-three books in two years is the real number, not the number I would have guessed.

## What it is not good for

Retrieval. A single line is nearly useless when I want to remember a specific argument. The notes I took while reading would be better for that — except I stopped taking them, and the log is what survived.

> The thing that survives is not the most thorough system. It is the one that costs almost nothing to keep.

## The format

```
2026-08-11 | The Craftsman | Skill grows from doing the same thing
                          | badly, repeatedly, with attention.
```

Three fields, one file, two years. It will probably outlast this blog.
